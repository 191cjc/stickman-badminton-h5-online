# Replay-anchor planning review

## Planning design review: acknowledgement mapping and pending edges

Reviewed current `design.md` section1 on 2026-10-15 before implementation. **The formula `B = L + (snapshot.tick - ackTick)` is conditional, not sufficient by itself to classify a local step as acknowledged.** `ack` is the confirmation boundary; the mapped tick only estimates which elapsed held steps the authority state already includes.

Counterexample: seq1 first affected local step1 and was incorporated at server tick1. The client then accepted seq2 (jump/hit/new direction) at local step2, but uplink stalled. A later downlink snapshot has tick50, ack1, ackTick1. B=50 overtakes the entire seq2 prediction interval even though seq2 is not acknowledged. Dropping all steps through B loses its edge. Conversely, restoring authority tick50 and blindly replaying every old seq2 movement step adds elapsed movement on top of time already represented by tick50; preserving an edge does not make this an exact temporal reconstruction.

### Rigorous first-release replay contract

1. **Separate command identity from elapsed steps.** Keep an accepted-command ledger keyed by seq, and a fixed-step ledger keyed by a monotonic local step. A step records held-state owner seq, the command seq(s) supplying each consumed edge, its eligible timing, and its relevant source context. Wire seq advances only on accepted send; local steps advance only from the shared fixed-time accumulator. Never infer acknowledgement solely from local time or mapped B.
2. **Coalesce pending commands at each fixed step exactly once.** Latest accepted held direction/jump/hitHeld wins; jumpPressed is OR over commands eligible before this step; use the most recent hitPressed edge and preserve that command's hit-time jump state through internal hitJump. Match server consumption semantics, including rapid hit+jump then release before a step. Mark consumed edges in the step ledger, not by mutating/removing the outstanding wire-command ledger. Multiple120-Hz commands before one60-Hz step must not overwrite an earlier edge or run extra physics. Later genuine edges remain individually identifiable if they occupied separate steps.
3. **Use mapped boundary only when its alignment is provable from retained history.** A known ack first-effective local step and valid numeric ackTick are necessary. A step can be pruned as represented only when **all** its command/edge owners are seq<=ack and it lies at/before B. If any step at/before B depends on seq>ack, or an unacknowledged command would have to be inserted before the new authoritative baseline, classify the mapping as inconsistent. Do not silently discard, relabel as acknowledged, or shift that unacknowledged edge behind a trim boundary.
4. **Normal aligned replay:** restore the authoritative player; replay retained fixed steps strictly after B in their original order, with exact1/60dt, held semantics and one-shot edge consumption. Drop acknowledged command identities but keep a compact copy of the known current ack anchor for future unchanged-ack snapshots. No packet-count stepping. Bound replay work/history as in section1. Matching a seq's first local step does not prove exact server consumption time for every prior coalesced edge; output remains prediction subject to authority correction.
5. **Explicit conservative fallback for null/missing/overtaking anchors:** restore a fresh authoritative player anchor and invalidate the unusable _step-time_ mapping, **without pretending outstanding seq>ack commands were confirmed**. Compact/rebase their still-outstanding operation batch onto the next eligible local fixed step using rule2, retaining latest held state, jump OR and latest hit edge/hit-time jump. This is a documented bounded fallback approximation, not recovery of the original historical timing; it may cause a correction, but cannot accelerate physics or lose the whole pending operation merely because the old ack is stale. Do not resend the commands or increment seq during reconstruction.
6. **Do not restart fallback edges on every unchanged-ack snapshot.** Retain a rebase generation keyed by semantic epoch + ack. After a fallback rebuild, ordinary fixed-time prediction consumes its compacted edges once in that generation. Further snapshots with the same overtaken ack can update read-only ball/context, score/status/effects and age diagnostics; do not rebuild/restart the own-player action from frame1 every30Hz. When ack advances, attempt alignment again and either reconcile normally or establish a new bounded rebase generation. Existing action latches remain authoritative; fallback cannot force-start a swing through an active one.
7. **Bounds and lifecycle dominate.** History overruns/stalls can suspend prediction and return to authority, but must be counted as an explicit prediction reset, not represented as a successful ack. Keep at most a compact pending-operation summary if the full ledger expires; never retain unbounded stalled history. Release/focus/room/score/phase/status resets intentionally cancel local operations through the existing lifecycle owner. A null ackTick by itself is not proof that every locally accepted seq>ack was discarded; only acknowledged seq or an explicit lifecycle cancellation gives that permission.

The simplest defensible implementation of this contract may conservatively use fallback whenever unacknowledged command time overlaps mapped B. Optimize the usual aligned case; do not add increasingly complicated affine tick mapping to hide asymmetric-stall ambiguity. An even smaller variant is to keep time-driven local stepping and **suspend own-player reconciliation for unchanged stale ack while unacknowledged commands overlap it**, until ack advances or a bounded stall/reset occurs. That avoids edge loss and double elapsed replay, but temporarily increases speculative local divergence and needs the same bounds/reset tests. Both choices preserve server authority; neither is exact rollback.

### Necessary adversarial tests for section1

- Asymmetric uplink pause while downlink snapshots continue: unchanged ack1/ackTick1 across many server ticks, pending seq2 hit+jump and held move. Its edge remains pending by identity, never disappears due B, and does not repeatedly restart on each snapshot. On delayed ack2, replay converges within the chosen bounded fallback contract.
- Two accepted commands between fixed steps: hit+jump press followed by release; latest held is false, but the jump/hit edges and hit-time jump survive to the same single source player step. Three commands include a second hit edge: newest hit context wins just as on server.
- Coalesced server ack names a seq with no independent local step, null metadata with outstanding seq>ack, stale unchanged ack after history eviction, tick-valid but overlapping anchors, and new numeric ack after fallback.
- Source latches plus long stalls: no replay can restart an active action or create multiple jumps, and no burst increases movement speed. Reconstructed edges may be executed once in each scratch rebuild but must not be appended to the live animation repeatedly for unchanged fallback generation.
- Separate diagnostic counters for aligned replay, overtaking/missing/null fallback, suspended prediction and history expiration. Bound all state and assert semantic reset clears generations and pending summaries.

### Assessment of amended fallback-generation proposal

The parent-session amendment is defensible for this release: mapped subtraction only trims confirmed-held steps; conflict/missing/null anchors restore once, rebase the outstanding compact operation batch into a next fixed step, then keep the same speculative generation until ack advances or an explicit reset/expiry occurs. No fatal server-authority concern was found, provided it is documented as bounded prediction rather than exact asymmetric-stall reconstruction.

Two details must be explicit in implementation/tests. First, expiry enters authority/suspended mode for the **same** generation; it must not create another generation under the same ack and replay an old hit every expiry interval. Second, newly accepted commands during an active fallback generation still enter normal next-step coalescing; protection against replay applies to the old rebased batch, not to future user input. Keep numeric→null ackTick transition separate from proof of acknowledgement: it may invalidate an anchor, but cannot acknowledge locally accepted seq>ack. Existing source latches may legitimately reject an edge in a rebuilt state; prediction must never force a swing/contact to preserve a prior visual expectation.
